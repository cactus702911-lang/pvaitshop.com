What Is a Facebook Video Selfie Verified Account?

To understand what sellers mean when they advertise these accounts, you first need to understand what Facebook's video selfie verification process actually is.

Facebook's Video Selfie Identity-Check Process

Facebook (owned by Meta) uses a range of identity verification methods to confirm that an account is operated by a real, living person — not a bot, automated script, or fake profile. The video selfie check is one of these methods. When triggered, it asks the account holder to record a short, rotating video of their face (a selfie video) so that Facebook's systems can compare it against the profile photo already associated with the account.

The video selfie check is performed entirely on-device and in real time. Its purpose is biometric confirmation: verifying that the person currently logging in or performing a certain action genuinely resembles the person who originally set up the account. Meta's systems analyze facial geometry, lighting, and liveness cues to detect spoofing attempts.

Why Does Meta Request a Video Selfie Verification?

Meta may trigger a video selfie verification request for a number of reasons, including:

  • Logging in from a new or unrecognized device or location
  • Unusual account activity patterns that suggest automated behavior
  • Reports from other users or Meta's own systems flagging the account
  • Attempting to create or manage a large number of ad campaigns in a short period
  • Recovering access to an account after a period of inactivity
  • Attempts to change sensitive account information such as the linked email or phone number

It is a security measure — not a one-time badge of achievement. An account that passed a video selfie check at some point in the past may be asked to pass it again at any time in the future.

Important Risks to Understand Before Buying

This is the most critical section of this guide. Before making any purchase decision, you must understand the following risks clearly and completely. No reputable seller can eliminate these risks — they are inherent to the nature of purchasing a third-party social media account.

Risk 1: The Original Owner May Retain Control or Recovery Access

Facebook's account recovery system is designed to return accounts to their original verified owners. Even if you have changed the password, email, and phone number associated with an account, the original owner may still be able to recover it by submitting identity documents or going through Facebook's trusted contacts recovery process. This is by design — Facebook's security infrastructure is built to protect users from account theft.

Risk 2: Account Credentials or Identity Information May Be Compromised

When you purchase an account from an unknown seller, you are receiving login credentials that the seller has had full access to. There is no way to verify whether the seller has stored these credentials elsewhere, shared them with others, or retained a recovery backdoor. The seller's operational security practices are entirely unknown to you.

Risk 3: Facebook May Request a Fresh Video Selfie Verification

Even if the account has previously passed a video selfie check, Meta's systems may request another one at any time — particularly when activity patterns change, the account logs in from a new device, or unusual behavior is detected. As a new operator of the account, you may find it impossible to pass this check if it requires matching the original owner's face.

Risk 4: Account Restrictions or Suspension May Occur

Facebook actively monitors accounts for signs of inauthentic behavior, including sudden changes in login location, device, or behavioral patterns. An account that changes hands may trigger automated review systems. Account restrictions, reduced feature access, advertising limitations, or outright suspension can occur — often without warning and sometimes without clear recourse.

Risk 5: Account Transfer May Conflict With Meta's Applicable Terms or Policies

Meta's Terms of Service specify that accounts are personal and non-transferable. While terms and their enforcement can evolve over time, buyers should be aware that account transfer practices may conflict with the platform's applicable policies. This is a risk you are accepting at your own discretion.

Risk 6: No Seller Can Guarantee Permanent Access or Future Verification Approval

Be cautious of any seller who claims to offer "permanent" access, "lifetime guarantees," or assurances that the account will never face another verification challenge. These claims cannot be substantiated. Meta controls the platform, and its systems can change at any time.

🚫 Caution: Do not share personal identity documents, government IDs, or your own video recordings with any third-party seller. No legitimate seller process requires you to submit your personal biometric data to them.

How to Evaluate Account-Related Security Risks

If, after understanding all of the above risks, you are still considering purchasing a Facebook account, here is a responsible checklist for evaluating the transaction and minimizing avoidable risks. This is not an endorsement of purchasing third-party accounts — it is practical guidance for those who have made or are seriously considering this decision.

Seller Evaluation Checklist

  • Verify the seller's business identity. Look for a verifiable business name, registered contact information, and a clear operational history. Anonymous sellers with no verifiable identity present heightened risk.
  • Review transparent terms and refund policies. A legitimate seller will have clear, written terms covering what you receive, what happens if the account is inaccessible, and what refund or replacement conditions apply.
  • Avoid sharing personal identity documents or sensitive information. No purchase process for a social media account should require you to submit government-issued ID, passport scans, or similar personal documents to the seller.
  • Never use another person's face, identity, or video selfie. If Facebook requests a video selfie verification, you must respond only with your own biometric data or decline and use official recovery options. Using another person's face would be identity fraud.
  • Do not trust claims of permanent verification or guaranteed access. These claims cannot be verified and are not under any seller's control.
  • Ask about the account's creation history. Understand how old the account is, whether it has been used for advertising, and whether it has any existing restrictions or violations.
  • Confirm support availability. Understand what support the seller offers after the sale, and for how long.

Facebook requests video selfie verification to confirm that an account is controlled by a real, living person rather than a bot or automated system.
